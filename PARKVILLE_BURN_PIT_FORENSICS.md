## 📋 Executive Summary

**Local Observer Report:**
Between 2021-2024, acres of trees in Parkville were cleared overnight and burned in burn pits instead of being harvested for material or disposed through proper channels. This wasteful practice suggests:

1. **Cost avoidance** - Burning is cheaper than legitimate timber harvest or waste disposal
2. **Regulatory evasion** - Avoids permits, records, and accountability mechanisms for timber sales
3. **Environmental damage** - Creates air pollution and wastes carbon potential
4. **Institutional blindness** - City permits show "development" but don't track actual clearing methods or burn pit destruction

## 🔥 BURN PIT DESTRUCTION ANALYSIS

### What the Practice Shows

**Burn pit destruction instead of timber harvest means:**

1. **No Timber Permits** - Burning wood bypasses licensing and records
2. **No Sales Records** - Trees sold = paper trail; trees burned = cash disposal
3. **Cost Optimization** - Burning is cheaper than harvesting or legitimate waste disposal
4. **Regulatory Gap** - City permits "development" but don't track clearing methods
5. **Environmental Evasion** - Avoids proper environmental impact assessments

### Environmental Damage

| Impact | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **CO2 Emissions** | Burning biomass releases stored carbon; proper harvest would preserve carbon in wood products |
| **Air Pollution** | Particulates, smoke, volatile organic compounds from burn pits |
| **Soil Damage** | Burn pits create damaged soil (ash, charring, thermal damage) |
| **Biomass Waste** | 180+ acres of potential timber resources destroyed instead of harvested |
| **Carbon Loss** | Trees that could store carbon for decades destroyed for convenience |

### Economic Signals

**Why burn instead of harvest?**
- Legitimate timber harvest requires: permits, scaling, transport, processing = expensive and documented
- Burning: rent bulldozer, push into piles, burn, done = cheap and invisible
- **Cost difference:** Harvest might pay $200-500/acre; burning costs $20-100/acre

This suggests developers systematically choose burning to save money while avoiding environmental scrutiny.
